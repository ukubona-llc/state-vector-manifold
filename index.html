The central finding

What xAI framed, Google computed, and OpenAI named precisely: this is not a classical Jacobian evaluated around a point. It is an epochal Jacobian — each column a historical regime transition, each perturbation a structural shock rather than an infinitesimal nudge. The manifold is moving. J(s,t) is itself a function of time.

The state equation ṡ = J(s,t)·s governs the evolution. A valuation model asks what s(t) is worth. The dynamical-systems formulation asks what governs ds/dt. The answer is the Jacobian — and as the state evolves, J changes regime. The 2023 system had a nearly diagonal J (one degree of freedom: demand). The 2028 system has a full, off-diagonal-rich J (four coupled degrees).

The Intel deal is an off-diagonal entry

OpenAI's sharpest observation: Intel is not an on-diagonal Jacobian entry. The diagonal J[L, L] represents compute scaling within the existing architecture. The off-diagonal J[L, w_Intel] represents a coupling between supplier allocation and compute labor that did not exist before. When that entry fires at 1.00 in 2026→27, the Jacobian's geometry changes. You cannot compress that away. It is a topological event in the manifold.

Intel is not increasing capacity directly. It is increasing capacity through resilience. When eigenvalues are distributed rather than concentrated, perturbations attenuate. A small shock to any one input in 2028 propagates through the entire coupled system and dissipates — rather than amplifying along a single concentrated eigenvector, as it would have in 2023.
